## Document 002: The School Board

Status: Draft

## Why This Document Exists in Civic Infrastructure

The public school system is the largest single consumer of property tax revenue
in Kane County, Illinois. It operates under a compulsory attendance mandate. Its
audience cannot consent, cannot leave, and has not yet developed the cognitive
architecture to evaluate what it is being given.

These three facts — largest extraction, compulsory attendance, captive audience
incapable of filtering — define the diagnostic frame for this document.

This is not a document about curriculum politics. It is a document about
structural design: who holds the authority, who bears the cost, what the
incentive structure produces, and why the outcome is so often pre-determined
before the affected person understands they are in a process at all.

The school board appears in this series for the same reason the HOA appeared in
Document 001: it holds real coercive authority over real people, it was designed
with accountability obligations lighter than its power, and the institutional
actors surrounding it have incentives that are aligned with each other and
opposed to the people paying for it.

The property taxpayer is the most affected person in this structure. They fund
it whether or not they use it, whether or not they have children, and whether or
not it produces the outcome it claims to produce. They have no ceiling above
them.

---

## The Extraction Structure

In Kane County, Illinois, the school district is the dominant consumer of
property tax revenue. This is not an estimate. It is arithmetic performed on
the official Kane County Clerk District Rate Listing, Tax Rates For 2024
Payable 2025, published May 5, 2025.

Across Kane County's school districts, the school levy consumes:

- **65.4%** of the total property tax bill in the St. Charles School District
  (District 303)
- **64.6%** in the Batavia School District (District 101)
- **74.0%** in the Kaneland Community Unit School District (District 302)

The range across Kane County runs from approximately 64% to 74% of the total
property tax bill, depending on location. Every other taxing body — the county
government, the municipality, the fire district, the library, the park district,
the forest preserve, the township — divides what remains.

There is no statutory ceiling on how high the school levy can go.

Illinois operates under the Property Tax Extension Limitation Law (PTELL), which
constrains the annual rate of growth for most taxing bodies. It is not a
ceiling. It is a governor on acceleration. And it has a bypass: a referendum.
The district calls the referendum. The district runs the public case for it.
The teachers union funds the yes campaign. The outcome is structurally
predictable.

The property taxpayer who does not use the school — who has no children, whose
children are grown, who sends children to private school, who moved in after the
last referendum — pays the full levy regardless. The tax does not follow the
child. The obligation follows the parcel.

This is the first diagnostic signal: **the school district extracts the majority
of the property tax burden from a population that has no individual ability to
decline, no ceiling above them, and no mechanism to recover their contribution
if the institution fails to produce its claimed outcome.**

## The Institution and Its Competitors

The public school system was built on a scarcity that no longer exists.

Its original justification rested on three pillars: knowledge was concentrated
in trained professionals, access to that knowledge required a physical structure,
and children needed to be in that structure to receive it. All three pillars
have collapsed.

**The Public Library** predates the Department of Education and has always
represented the democratic alternative. It is self-directed, free,
non-compulsory, and non-ideological by design. It trusts the patron to know
what they need. It does not assign, grade, or fail. It does not require
attendance. It has survived for over a century as civic infrastructure without
a compulsory funding mandate attached to every parcel of land in its district.

**Online learning** has eliminated the information scarcity argument entirely.
Khan Academy, MIT OpenCourseWare, YouTube, and thousands of specialized
platforms have made the actual content of education — including advanced STEM
content that most school districts cannot staff — freely available to anyone
with an internet connection. The knowledge is no longer in the building.

**On-the-job training** is the most structurally devastating competitor because
it produces the outcome the institution claims to produce — a capable,
employable adult — without the institution. The quiet but accelerating erosion
of degree requirements at major employers over the last decade is not a trend.
It is a diagnostic signal. The labor market is telling the institution that its
primary credential is losing exchange value.

An institution that is genuinely useful does not need to mandate its own
consumption. Compulsory attendance laws, truancy enforcement, accreditation
gatekeeping, and credential requirements for employment are not the
infrastructure of education. They are the infrastructure of a monopoly
defending its market position against competitors that are demonstrably capable
of replacing it.

This is the second diagnostic signal: **the public school system's most
durable structural advantage is not the quality of its educational output. It
is the legal compulsion that delivers a captive audience and a captive funding
stream regardless of that output.**

## The Institutional Actors and Their Incentives

The school board is elected. This is the beginning and the end of its
democratic accountability mechanism.

Board elections occur on off-cycle dates with low turnout. The board controls
the agenda of its own meetings. It hires the superintendent. The superintendent
hires the administrators. The administrators implement the curriculum. The
teachers union negotiates the contract that governs the teachers who deliver it.

Each institutional actor's incentives are worth examining individually.

**The School Board** is composed of elected volunteers who hold authority over
a budget that, in Kane County, routinely exceeds hundreds of millions of dollars
per district. No professional qualification is required to serve. No financial
competence examination is administered. Board members face no personal liability
for budget decisions. The institution's insurance covers them. Their primary
accountability is electoral — and the electorate that shows up to school board
elections is not representative of the property taxpayer population bearing the
cost.

**The Superintendent** is a professional administrator hired by and accountable
to the board. Superintendent compensation in Illinois school districts is public
record and is frequently in the range of $200,000 to $300,000 or more annually,
including benefits, in Kane County districts. The superintendent's incentive is
institutional continuation and expansion. A superintendent who proposes reducing
the district's scope, outsourcing functions to more efficient providers, or
acknowledging that competitors have surpassed the institution in specific
domains does not remain a superintendent.

**The Teachers Union** is the most organized and consistently present
institutional actor in the structure. It negotiates contracts, funds referendum
campaigns, endorses board candidates, and maintains a political presence that
the individual property taxpayer does not. The union's institutional interest is
compensation, benefits, job security, and working conditions for its members.
These are legitimate interests. They are not the same as the educational outcome
of the student.

**The Administrators** are the layer between the board and the classroom. They
implement curriculum frameworks, manage compliance requirements, and produce the
institutional output — reports, assessments, improvement plans — that justifies
the administrative layer's own existence. Administrative growth in public school
districts has consistently outpaced enrollment growth for decades. This is not
an accident. It is what institutions do when they are not subject to market
discipline.

**The Property Taxpayer** funds all of it. They have one formal recourse: the
ballot, in an election they are statistically unlikely to vote in, for
candidates they are unlikely to know, in a process the institution's most
organized constituency controls.

This is the third diagnostic signal: **every institutional actor in the school
district structure has aligned incentives that point toward institutional
growth, continuation, and cost expansion. None of those incentives are
structurally tied to the educational outcome of the student or the financial
interest of the property taxpayer.**

## The Compulsory Captive Audience

Children are compelled to attend. This is not incidental to the analysis. It is
the structural fact that changes everything else.

In a market, a consumer who receives a poor product stops buying it. In the
public school system, the consumer cannot stop. Truancy is a legal offense.
The child must be present. The parent must deliver them. The tax must be paid.

This would be an acceptable arrangement if the institution's monopoly on the
audience were matched by a monopoly on the quality of outcome. It is not. The
competitors described above — the library, online learning, on-the-job training
— produce measurable outcomes that the institution increasingly cannot claim
superiority over, particularly in STEM domains where established, testable,
falsifiable knowledge is the measure.

There is a category distinction that the institution has progressively collapsed:
the distinction between established knowledge and contested theory.

The Laws of Physics are not a matter of perspective. The rules of arithmetic
are not culturally constructed. The periodic table does not have a political
valence. These are the domains in which the institution's original value
proposition was clearest — conveying the accumulated, tested, falsifiable
knowledge of civilization to the next generation.

The progressive substitution of contested social and political theory into the
curriculum — presented with the same institutional authority as established
fact, to an audience that has not yet developed the cognitive architecture to
distinguish between them — is not an educational choice. It is an institutional
survival choice.

A child who has mastered algebra, physics, chemistry, history, and rhetoric can
evaluate contested claims independently. An institution that produces that child
has made itself partially replaceable. An institution that produces a child who
has been given conclusions rather than the tools to reach conclusions has
created a different kind of dependency.

This is the fourth diagnostic signal: **the compulsory captive audience is the
institution's last structural moat. The collapse of the distinction between
established knowledge and contested theory is what is being done with it.**

## The Accountability Gap

The property taxpayer who believes the school district is extracting too much,
producing too little, or operating outside its proper educational scope has the
following formal remedies available:

- Comment at a public board meeting, subject to time limits set by the board
- Vote in the next board election, against candidates supported by the most
  organized constituency in the district
- Appeal a property tax assessment, which affects the assessed value but not
  the levy rate
- Move

These are not equivalent remedies. They are a list that creates the appearance
of recourse while the structure remains unchanged.

The Illinois State Board of Education provides a regulatory layer above the
local board. It sets curriculum frameworks, administers accreditation, and
distributes state funding. It does not discipline local boards for financial
mismanagement in any consistent way. It does not intervene when a district's
administrative cost growth outpaces its educational output. It does not restore
money to the property taxpayer when the institution fails to deliver its claimed
outcome.

The courts are available. Litigation against a school district in Illinois
follows a pattern that will be familiar from Document 001: the district litigates
from public funds, the taxpayer litigates from personal funds, and the process
is designed to be slow enough that most people stop before they finish.

This is the fifth diagnostic signal: **the formal accountability mechanisms
available to the property taxpayer are procedurally present and structurally
ineffective. The institution is insulated from consequence by the same legal
framework that grants it the authority to extract.**

## The Existential Condition

Institutions that are genuinely necessary do not need to mandate their own
consumption, suppress their competitors, or convert their captive audience into
advocates for their own continuation.

Institutions that are in existential struggle do all three.

The Department of Education at the federal level, and its state and local
counterparts, exhibit each of these behaviors. Federal education funding
creates dependency that makes local districts resistant to structural change.
Accreditation systems create credential requirements that make institutional
alternatives difficult to recognize. Curriculum frameworks that emphasize social
formation over knowledge transmission create graduates whose primary civic
identity is tied to the institution's preferred conclusions.

None of this requires a conspiracy. It requires only that the people running
the institution are human beings who believe in what they are doing, are
financially dependent on the institution continuing, and are surrounded by
colleagues who share both the belief and the dependency.

The smoking bearing does not need a villain. It needs heat, friction, and the
absence of a mechanism to stop.

## What Civic Infrastructure Does With This

The Diagnostic Surface for the school district is built on the same foundation
as the HOA surface: affected-status qualification, evidence discipline, and
durable publication outside the institution's control.

The property taxpayer has standing. The tax bill is the evidence anchor. The
levy rate is a public record. The budget is a public record. The superintendent's
compensation is a public record. The referendum history is a public record.
The test score trajectory is a public record.

None of these records, individually, tell the complete story. Assembled,
qualified, and placed in a diagnostic surface that survives the institution's
preferred narrative, they do.

The person who has been paying 65% to 74% of their property tax bill to a school
district for twenty years, whose neighborhood school has a declining STEM
performance record, whose board just passed a referendum with 34% voter turnout,
and who has nowhere to put what they know — that person has standing.

The Diagnostic Surface gives them somewhere real to put it.
