## What This Series Is

This is not a policy paper. It is not a reform agenda. It is not a complaint.

It is a diagnostic series.

Each document in this series examines one institution, one structure, or one
system — not to condemn it, not to propose replacing it, but to explain in
plain terms why it functions the way it does. Why the friction exists. Why the
outcomes are so often pre-determined before the weaker party enters the process.
Why the people closest to the problem are the least equipped to change it.

The series title is deliberate. A hitchhiker does not own the vehicle. They did
not design the road. They are along for the ride, observing, and they would
very much like to understand why the engine is making that sound before it
becomes relevant to their immediate survival.

Nothing ever works without friction. Our civilization barely functions. This
series explains why, one institution at a time, with as much precision and as
little rage as the subject permits.

---

## How to Read This Series

Each document follows the same diagnostic logic:

- What is the institution, legally and mechanically?
- Who are the institutional actors, and what are their incentives?
- Where does the legal framework place the burden, and why?
- Where is the structural pre-determination most visible?
- What does this pattern have in common with the others?
- What does Civic Infrastructure do with it?

No document in this series requires you to have read any other. Each stands
alone as a diagnostic record of its subject. The series as a whole reveals the
pattern that repeats across all of them.

## The Candidate List

The following are the subjects identified for this series. They are grouped by
domain. They share a common structural pattern: authority without proportional
accountability, incentives misaligned with the people subject to that authority,
and outcomes structurally pre-determined before the weaker party enters the
process.

This list is not exhaustive. It is a starting inventory. Subjects will be added
as the series develops.

## The Repeating Pattern

Across every domain in this list, the same structure appears in different dress:

1. Authority is delegated to a private or quasi-private body.
2. The body's accountability obligations are lighter than its power.
3. The institutional actors surrounding the body have aligned incentives that
   differ from the people subject to the body's authority.
4. The legal framework reflects the influence of organized institutional
   interests, not the affected population.
5. The formal remedies available to the affected person are expensive, slow,
   and structurally disadvantaged.

This is not a conspiracy. It does not require bad actors, though bad actors
thrive in it. It requires only that the people with the most to gain from the
structure are the most present when the structure is designed — and that the
people most affected by it are the least organized to change it.

## The Diagnostic Purpose

This series exists because the Civic Infrastructure model requires Diagnostic
Surfaces to be built for something real.

The most widely distributed civic failure in America today is not corruption in
the conventional sense. It is the quiet, structural, daily pre-determination of
outcomes for the people least equipped to change them — in their HOA, in their
school district, in their hospital bill, in their lease, in their arbitration
clause, in their credit report.

These failures are visible. The affected person sees them. They record them,
post about them, search for others who have experienced the same thing. What
they lack is a surface where that observation becomes a durable, qualified,
legible record — one that survives the institution's preferred version of
events.

That is what this series builds toward. Not reform. Not revolution.

A record that holds.
