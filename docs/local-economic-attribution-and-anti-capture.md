# Local Economic Attribution and Anti-Capture

Status: Draft  
Repository: `civic-affordance-model`  
Scope: Civic Affordances Diagnostics

## Purpose

This document defines how local economic activity, contributor attribution, and anti-capture policy fit inside Civic Infrastructure.

Civic Infrastructure is not anti-commerce. It is anti-capture.

## Core Principle

Civic Infrastructure is not required to be free of cost. It is required to remain locally grounded.

A county implementation may involve ordinary local commerce, contracts, technical work, hosting costs, consulting, paid administration, donated labor, local business support, charitable contribution, or other local arrangements.

Those arrangements do not create purchased entitlement to cooperative recognition.

## Local Commerce Is Allowed

A local actor may pay for work.

Examples:

- a local business hires a technical operator to deploy a county-state surface;
- a local charity pays for hosting;
- a local professional funds a diagnostic surface related to professional competence;
- a root charges for setup, maintenance, or support;
- a local organization pays for DNS, mail, Fediverse, directory, WireGuard, DANE, or attestation infrastructure;
- a contributor buys IP addresses, servers, electricity, backups, monitoring, or administrative time.

The model does not impose policy restrictions on commerce inside an independent county implementation.

Each county root is locally owned and locally accountable.

## Civic Infrastructure Does Not Collect Money in Its Own Name

The cooperative network should not become a centralized fundraising vehicle, paid listing service, sponsorship marketplace, advertising exchange, franchise system, or vendor channel.

Contributors bring their own resources:

- technical expertise;
- hosting;
- hardware;
- public addresses;
- domain names;
- electricity;
- administration;
- documentation;
- local knowledge;
- time and effort.

In return, the contributor may receive visible attribution for the infrastructure they operate or support.

This resembles operator or node attribution. It is not a sale of network authority.

## Local Attribution

Local attribution may identify the contributor responsible for a county-state surface or specific interface.

Attribution may include:

- an individual operator;
- a local business;
- a charity;
- a local technical group;
- a professional practice;
- a civic project;
- another locally grounded contributor identity.

Attribution should remain tied to local standing or direct operational contribution.

## Attribution Is Not General Advertising

Local attribution is not a purchasable advertising product.

A contributor may be visible because the contributor operates, funds, hosts, maintains, or locally supports infrastructure.

A remote actor should not be able to purchase attention inside a county surface merely because the surface exists.

## Local Standing Requirement

Economic visibility belongs to local contribution and local standing.

A sawmill in the same county may be visible in that county's civic infrastructure when it has local standing or direct contribution.

A sawmill in another county, state, country, continent, planet, or galaxy has no automatic claim to visibility inside that county's civic infrastructure.

This restriction protects the infrastructure from becoming a scalable advertising surface for outside economic actors.

## Anti-Capture Boundary

Civic Infrastructure must be resistant to capture by remote corporations, national sponsors, outside vendors, centralized funders, astroturf campaigns, or actors attempting to simulate local standing.

Capture indicators may include:

- outside actors purchasing local-looking surfaces;
- remote corporations funding county surfaces without local accountability;
- repeated identical surfaces across counties under centralized control;
- contributor attribution that hides the real controlling party;
- paid visibility not tied to local standing or direct operation;
- attempts to convert cooperation into a franchise, ad network, or vendor program;
- claims that payment entitles the contributor to DNS recognition, federation, peering, routing, or trust.

These indicators do not automatically prove misconduct. They are diagnostic signals.

## Cooperative Judgment

Other roots may judge whether a contribution is locally grounded, authentic, useful, coherent, captured, astroturfed, or fraudulent.

A local root may disagree with another root's judgment.

The network does not need a central enforcement body for this. Roots may simply cooperate, partially cooperate, decline, fork, or isolate.

## Purchased Entitlement Is Rejected

Payment does not buy cooperation.

A contributor may pay for implementation. A root may charge for work. A business may fund its own infrastructure. A local actor may hire someone to plug them in.

None of that obligates other roots to:

- resolve DNS;
- publish delegation;
- federate;
- peer over WireGuard;
- accept directory data;
- exchange mail;
- rely on DANE records;
- mirror attestations;
- expose attribution;
- treat the surface as authentic.

Cooperation remains elective.

## Isolation

If a contributor is astroturfing, falsely claiming local standing, or attempting to defraud the Civic Infrastructure network, cooperating roots may remove or decline the DNS cooperation layer and any other interface cooperation.

The implementation may continue to exist locally.

The result is isolation, not central punishment. The surface may remain online, but the cooperative network does not have to route, resolve, federate, peer, query, trust, mirror, or recognize it.

## Diagnostic Value of Capture Attempts

Capture attempts are not merely threats. They are diagnostic signals.

If an outside actor attempts to purchase, distort, centralize, spoof, or occupy a county surface, that attempt reveals something about the civic, economic, technical, and institutional environment around the infrastructure.

The model should preserve such signals where appropriate. It should not hide them merely because they are uncomfortable.

## Policy Summary

Civic Infrastructure is receptive to local commerce.

Civic Infrastructure is resistant to extra-county capture.

Local economic activity may support infrastructure.

Remote purchase does not create local standing.

Attribution may recognize contribution.

Attribution must not become a cross-county advertising network.

Cooperation is earned by the surface, interface, conduct, and local grounding, not bought.
