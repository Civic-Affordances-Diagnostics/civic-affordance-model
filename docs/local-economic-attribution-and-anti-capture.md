# Local Economic Attribution and Anti-Capture

Status: Draft  
Repository: `civic-affordance-model`  
Scope: Civic Affordances Diagnostics

## Purpose

This document defines how local commerce, contributor attribution, and
anti-capture policy fit the Civic Affordance Diagnostics model.

It separates three things that must not be confused:

1. local commerce inside an independent county implementation;
2. visible attribution for local contribution;
3. cooperative recognition by other roots.

## Core Principle

Civic Infrastructure is receptive to local commerce and resistant to
extra-county capture.

Local economic activity may support infrastructure. Remote purchase does not
create local standing or cooperative recognition.

## Local Commerce Is Allowed

Civic Infrastructure is not required to be free of cost.

A county implementation may include ordinary local commerce, including:

- a local business hires a root or technical operator;
- a local resident contracts for implementation support;
- a local charity pays for hosting;
- a local professional funds a diagnostic surface related to professional
  competence;
- a root charges for setup, maintenance, or support;
- a local organization pays for DNS, mail, Fediverse, directory, WireGuard,
  DANE, or attestation infrastructure;
- a contributor buys IP addresses, servers, electricity, backups, monitoring,
  or administrative time.

The model does not impose policy restrictions on commerce inside an independent
county implementation.

## Cooperative Recognition Is Not Sold

Local commerce does not bind the cooperative network.

Payment, contract, sponsorship, hosting, consulting, donation, or local
commercial arrangement does not create entitlement to:

- DNS recognition;
- federation;
- WireGuard peering;
- directory exchange;
- mail exchange;
- DANE reliance;
- attestation mirroring;
- interface publication;
- local attribution outside the contributor's own surface.

Cooperation remains elective.

## Civic Infrastructure Does Not Collect Money in Its Own Name

The cooperative network should not become a centralized fundraising vehicle,
paid listing service, sponsorship marketplace, advertising exchange, franchise
system, or vendor channel.

Contributors bring their own resources:

- technical expertise;
- hosting;
- hardware;
- public addresses;
- domain names;
- electricity;
- administration;
- documentation;
- local knowledge;
- time and effort.

In return, the contributor may receive visible attribution for the
infrastructure they operate or support.

This resembles operator or node attribution. It is not a sale of network
authority.

## Local Attribution

Local attribution may identify the contributor responsible for a county-state
surface or specific interface.

Attribution may include:

- an individual operator;
- a local business;
- a charity;
- a local technical group;
- a professional practice;
- a civic project;
- another locally grounded contributor identity.

Attribution should remain tied to local standing or direct operational
contribution.

## Attribution Is Not General Advertising

Local attribution is not a purchasable advertising product.

A contributor may be visible because the contributor operates, funds, hosts,
maintains, or locally supports infrastructure.

A remote actor should not be able to purchase attention inside a county surface
merely because the surface exists.

## Local Standing Requirement

Economic visibility belongs to local contribution and local standing.

A sawmill in the same county may be visible in that county's civic
infrastructure when it has local standing or direct contribution.

A sawmill in another county, state, country, continent, planet, or galaxy has no
automatic claim to visibility inside that county's civic infrastructure.

This restriction protects the infrastructure from becoming a scalable
advertising surface for outside economic actors.

## Anti-Capture Boundary

Civic Infrastructure must be resistant to capture by remote corporations,
national sponsors, outside vendors, centralized funders, astroturf campaigns, or
actors attempting to simulate local standing.

Capture indicators may include:

- outside actors purchasing local-looking surfaces;
- remote corporations funding county surfaces without local accountability;
- repeated identical surfaces across counties under centralized control;
- contributor attribution that hides the real controlling party;
- paid visibility not tied to local standing or direct operation;
- attempts to convert cooperation into a franchise, ad network, or vendor
  program;
- claims that payment entitles the contributor to DNS recognition, federation,
  peering, routing, or trust.

These indicators do not automatically prove misconduct. They are diagnostic
signals.

## Cooperative Judgment

Other roots may judge whether a contribution is locally grounded, authentic,
useful, coherent, captured, astroturfed, or fraudulent.

A local root may disagree with another root's judgment.

The network does not need a central enforcement body for this. Roots may simply
cooperate, partially cooperate, decline, fork, or isolate.

## Isolation

If a contributor is astroturfing, falsely claiming local standing, or attempting
to defraud the Civic Infrastructure network, cooperating roots may remove or
decline the DNS cooperation layer and any other interface cooperation.

The implementation may continue to exist locally.

The result is isolation, not central punishment. The surface may remain online,
but the cooperative network does not have to route, resolve, federate, peer,
query, trust, mirror, or recognize it.

## Diagnostic Value of Capture Attempts

Capture attempts are not merely threats. They are diagnostic signals.

If an outside actor attempts to purchase, distort, centralize, spoof, or occupy
a county surface, that attempt reveals something about the civic, economic,
technical, and institutional environment around the infrastructure.

The model should preserve such signals where appropriate. It should not hide
them merely because they are uncomfortable.
